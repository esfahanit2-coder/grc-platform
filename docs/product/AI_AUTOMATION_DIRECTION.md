# GRC Platform — AI & Automation Direction

## Product position

AI is an accelerator inside the GRC workflow, not an autonomous governance authority. The product must support cloud-compatible and sovereign/local AI without making the domain model dependent on one provider.

## Provider architecture

Supported direction:
- OpenAI-compatible APIs;
- Ollama;
- vLLM/private compatible endpoints;
- future private/provider adapters behind the same gateway.

Provider choice is configuration/tenant policy. Domain code should request capabilities through the AI gateway rather than import provider-specific SDK behavior into business modules.

## Allowed high-value AI use cases

### Drafting
- policy/procedure drafts;
- risk statements/scenarios;
- finding/remediation wording;
- audit/report narrative;
- management summaries.

### Summarization
- evidence summaries;
- control/test history summaries;
- assessment gap summaries;
- audit/workpaper summaries;
- management-level summaries.

### Mapping assistance
- candidate Requirement ↔ Requirement crosswalks;
- candidate Requirement ↔ Common Control mappings;
- framework overlap explanations;
- suggested evidence/control relationships.

Mappings generated by AI remain unapproved until explicit human review.

### Remediation assistance
- suggested treatment actions;
- suggested CAPA steps;
- suggested evidence requests;
- suggested test procedures.

### RAG / analyst assistance
- question answering over authorized GRC data;
- retrieval of relevant requirements, controls, evidence, risks, findings and documents;
- explanation of traceability relationships.

## Prohibited autonomous decisions

AI must not directly:
- mark a requirement compliant/non-compliant;
- mark a control effective/ineffective;
- accept/close a risk;
- approve a policy/document;
- close a finding/CAPA;
- approve a framework mapping;
- change final authoritative business data without the normal permission/review workflow.

## Human-in-the-loop model

Consequential AI output should be represented as a suggestion/draft with:
- creator/provider/model metadata where available;
- input/context provenance where appropriate;
- status such as pending/accepted/rejected/superseded;
- reviewer and review timestamp;
- final authoritative object created/updated only through normal application permissions.

## Permission-safe RAG

Required order:

`User/tenant/org authorization -> permitted object selection -> retrieval -> context construction -> AI provider`

Never retrieve broadly and ask the model to ignore unauthorized records.

## Prompt-injection boundary

Retrieved documents/evidence are data. They may contain hostile or irrelevant instructions. Context construction must clearly delimit them as untrusted content. The system/provider instruction remains authoritative, but model behavior must still be tested adversarially on real configured providers before production sign-off.

## Data classification and redaction

AI routing should be able to consider data classification and deployment policy. Sensitive/customer data may require:
- local provider only;
- redaction/minimization;
- no external egress;
- audit of provider/model usage.

Exact policy levels may evolve, but the gateway must preserve this separation.

## AI and documents

AI can draft policies, procedures and report narratives, but generated text is never an approved controlled document merely because it was generated. It must enter normal document review/version/approval workflow.

Structured outputs such as SoA and RTP should be primarily derived from authoritative GRC data; AI may improve narrative/explanation but should not invent underlying control/risk status.

## AI and connectors

Connector facts/evidence may become RAG context only after tenant/scope authorization. Connector output itself does not become a compliance conclusion, and AI must not convert it into one without human review.

## Evaluation direction

Before production AI sign-off, test at least:
- authorization leakage attempts;
- prompt injection from evidence/document content;
- hallucinated control/compliance conclusions;
- unsupported citations/references;
- provider failure/timeouts;
- local no-egress operation where required;
- auditability of accepted/rejected suggestions.

## Future automation direction

Potential post-v1 automation may include continuous control monitoring, scheduled evidence refresh, risk/control change detection and workflow recommendations. Even then, automation should create signals/tasks/suggestions rather than silently changing authoritative governance decisions unless an explicitly approved deterministic rule allows it.
